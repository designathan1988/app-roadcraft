# Roadcraft: authoring, agents, and performance direction

This decision record follows an interactive browser pass on the integrated game. A player could draw a raised road and a polygon building, but the building controls competed with the map, the road height tool did not explain when a cut becomes a tunnel, and vehicles had no destination beyond a short route horizon. The design below is the target for follow-on implementation, not a claim that every capability is already present.

## Reproducible integration baseline

The integrated preview runs at `http://127.0.0.1:5190/` from the `codex/roadcraft-integrated` worktree. Browser checks used a 1280 × 800 desktop viewport and a 390 × 844 mobile viewport. The legacy `grid-and-bends.json` map loaded with 38 roads, 16 vehicles, and 41 visible people and no page errors. Building checks drew four- and six-corner footprints on the terrain, edited floors, facade, and roof, then confirmed undo, redo, save, and load. Road checks placed a raised span at +8 m and a tunnel at −8 m; the renderer contained pier and portal geometry respectively. Page Up changed the uncommitted endpoint preview by 1 m, and placement released the drawing tool. A separately inserted road height point stayed editable and survived save/load.

Visual verification passed 16 scenes with no page errors, non-finite vertices, or buried road surface outside tunnels. A small fuzz hunt (6 seeds × 24 operations) reports only known open `turnOffSurface` and `bodyOverlap` categories. The required 80 × 40 hunt, complete `npm run check`, and final combined browser flow remain gates.

An 80 × 40 world-only hunt on 2026-09-28, one worker and below-normal priority, completed before the building-access graph change. It reported 5,039 `turnOffSurface` occurrences, 10 `surfaceGap`, 5 `elevationStep`, and 2 geometry exceptions. The same hunt after the access change produced an identical tally and report. The latter three categories are release blockers, not passed checks. The reports are `roadcraft-fuzz-before-20260928.txt` and `roadcraft-fuzz-after-access-20260928.txt` in the OS temp directory. A full pedestrian suite was stopped when whole-machine CPU reached 85%; it needs a bounded runner or a smaller case before repeating. Targeted building-access, destination and crossing-containment tests passed. An isolated browser fixture placed a house beside a road and showed 12 people with zero page errors. On the 186-segment headless benchmark, updating building entrances after placement took a median 9.47 ms and did not rebuild vehicle topology. Full public-ground navigation and the final combined browser gate remain open.

The road geometry pass replaced an unreliable all-or-nothing Boolean call with a compatible Martinez result and a Clipper2 fallback only for kernel failures. Clipper2 alone removed a valid 1.35-unit patch of asphalt on one acute junction, so it cannot replace the existing fill semantics globally. A separate fix chooses a junction plate when a width transition is shortened below 75% of its designed run; a short elevated link now limits the adjacent deck to the height its ramp can reach. The latest 80 × 40 world-only hunt reports 5,063 `turnOffSurface` occurrences, 3 `surfaceGap`, 0 `elevationStep`, and 0 exceptions (`roadcraft-fuzz-after-road-fixes-20260928.txt` in the OS temp directory). The three remaining surface gaps and vehicle path departures are open release blockers. A full `npm run check` attempt passed lint and typecheck, then its coverage suite was interrupted after a long single-worker run because the targeted `pedestrian-crossing-seed3` regression is independently known to fail. That pedestrian becomes trapped near the far kerb by a bus stopped across the crossing; the crossing-entry check alone does not prevent a vehicle from occupying it later.

The `pedestrian-crossing-seed3` stall is now fixed at its physical cause. On a three-leg junction, a bus halted for a red signal with its front across the zebra of an adjacent leg. The topology build now measures that adjacent zebra against each incoming lane and caches an earlier safe signal stop. The fixture and all 20 active fuzz regressions pass; 12 focused junction/collision tests pass. An A/B headless benchmark on the same 186-segment map measured 2.908 ms/tick before and 2.911 ms/tick after. The new crossing measurement made vehicle topology about 29 ms slower in this short three-edit sample (275 to 304 ms); total topology timings varied in the opposite direction (621 to 608 ms), so a longer benchmark is needed before claiming a net edit-latency change.

A curved ribbon had a further 0.36-unit hole where its offset side missed the straight chord of a two-leg junction mouth. Each curved leg now contributes a four-unit sampled overlap around its mouth, at the same half-width as its road level. The shrunk `curved-mouth-ribbon-gap` fixture passes. The post-change 80 × 40 world hunt found 0 `surfaceGap`, 0 `elevationStep`, and 0 exceptions, while `turnOffSurface` remained 5,063 in all 80 sequences. The full 16-scenario WebGL visual verifier passed again, with no page errors, non-finite mesh vertices, or asphalt below terrain outside tunnel bores. Turn trajectories remain the road release blocker.

A representative two-leg bend showed that the existing single cubic put even its centreline 2.7 units outside asphalt; changing its one handle length did not solve it. A tangent-continuous pair of cubics through a searched point inside the junction fit the heaviest vehicle on that reduced case. This fallback runs only after the standard handle candidates fail and is memoised with the same geometry digest. The 80 × 40 hunt reduced `turnOffSurface` from 5,063 to 4,317 occurrences, with the other world categories still at zero. The 21 active fuzz regressions, 13 targeted collision/signal/class-join tests, and 16 WebGL visual scenes passed. Most remaining departures occur in complex five- and six-leg junctions; the road milestone remains open. Class-aware movement eligibility and a general bounded path planner are still needed before this category can pass, without widening road surfaces just to hide the defect.

Connector paths now record the largest swept body class they physically contain. Spawned vehicles, fixed-destination routing, fallback routing, replanning and junction admission exclude connectors below that vehicle's class; an entry unable to carry the sampled archetype does not spawn it. The world detector still reports movements that fit no body at all, rather than counting buses on turns they will never take. The latest 80 × 40 hunt reported 1,178 such `turnOffSurface` cases in 77 sequences and zero surface, elevation, exception or trim-order faults. The comparison with the earlier 4,317 count uses a different eligibility criterion, so it is not a like-for-like improvement claim. On the final seed-1 map, 20 connectors fit no body, while all 151 connectors marked usable passed the size-specific surface check. The unusable movements remain a release blocker and need explicit editor feedback or geometric repair.

Pedestrian crossing placement now checks that both kerb endpoints are on the actual footway. It searches only within the space left after the opposite junction mouth and a minimum drivable link are reserved; if no safe location fits, it does not paint or route that crossing. Reservations and vehicle yielding measure every crossing of the local node that a body physically sweeps, including an adjacent leg, and the pedestrian admission audit shares the same rear-clearance rule. The previously failing `pedestrian-vehicle-body-seed5`, `pedestrian-reservation-seed6`, and the three-operation opposing-mouth fixture pass. Twenty-three active fuzz regressions pass; two old collision fixtures now pass as normal tests. The acute bus turn remains the one expected failure. A short 186-segment benchmark measured 1.60 ms per tick and 565 ms simulation topology rebuild; these timings vary with machine load and do not demonstrate the requested regional rebuild yet.

## Reference study and decisions

| Need | Reference behavior | Roadcraft decision |
|---|---|---|
| Start a building quickly | [The Sims 4](https://cdn-assets-ts4.pulse.ea.com/Guide/TheSims4_PlayersGuide_ENGLISH.pdf) moves and resizes whole rooms with on-screen handles. | Offer a one-gesture starter shape and a free outline on the live terrain. Both become the same editable building. |
| Make any mass precisely | [SketchUp](https://help.sketchup.com/en/sketchup/drawing-basic-shapes) uses inference and numeric dimensions; [Shapr3D](https://support.shapr3d.com/hc/en-us/articles/18857081591964-Sketching-in-Shapr3D) keeps sketches editable while extruding them. | A selected footprint, wall, roof, or tier gets a direct handle and nearby measurement input. No permanent tray of unrelated fields. |
| Add detail without repetitive placement | [Tiny Glade](https://store.steampowered.com/app/2198150/Tiny_Glade/) derives doors and support details from drawn structures. | Generate a coherent default facade and roof from the mass. Clicking a face exposes only that face's pattern, openings, and material controls. Manual overrides survive shape edits. |
| Draw a road without guessing its shape | [Cities: Skylines II](https://www.paradoxinteractive.com/games/cities-skylines-ii/features/road-tools) previews snapping, slope, cut, and elevation. [Transport Fever 2](https://www.wiki.transportfever2.com/doku.php?id=gamemanual:streetstracks) lets players adjust an endpoint after tracing and proposes bridges or tunnels from clearance. | Trace horizontally, then adjust the selected endpoint or an intermediate profile handle before committing. Keep the road visible in the world throughout. Show the proposed junction, slope, clearance, and structure along the whole preview. |
| Make vertical design comprehensible | [Civil 3D](https://help.autodesk.com/cloudhelp/2023/ENU/Civil3D-UserGuide/files/GUID-F1C4C2F3-3B0A-40AC-8B92-2D670D3A50E9.htm) relates a horizontal alignment to the ground and finished-grade profiles. | Store one continuous vertical profile per authored alignment; derive ramp, cut, bridge, and tunnel spans from profile versus terrain. A small contextual profile appears during editing, without making the player use a separate structure mode. |
| Give traffic a purpose | [SUMO](https://sumo.dlr.de/docs/Simulation/Routing.html) separates destinations and rerouting from local lane behavior. | Assign every spawned vehicle a reachable destination. Plan a network route with congestion and turn costs, then reconsider at bounded events, preserving junction reservations. |
| Let people reach places | [JuPedSim](https://www.jupedsim.org/stable/concepts/routing.html) separates a journey's goal/stages from wayfinding through walkable geometry. [Recast/DetourCrowd](https://github.com/recastnavigation/recastnavigation/blob/main/DetourCrowd/Include/DetourCrowd.h) separates path corridors from local crowd avoidance. | Join sidewalks, crossings, public open ground, and building entrances into one navigation topology. Give a person a destination and activity; let the local movement solver avoid people and furniture while staying in walkable space. |
| Make intersections respond | [SUMO](https://sumo.dlr.de/docs/Simulation/Traffic_Lights.html) extends or skips phases based on measured demand. | Keep the conflict matrix as the safety authority; choose phase timing from queues and waiting pedestrians, with minimum green and clearance time. |
| Stay fluid | [Three.js](https://threejs.org/docs/pages/InstancedMesh.html) uses instancing to reduce draw calls; its [optimization guide](https://threejs.org/manual/pages/optimize-lots-of-objects.html) explains batching. [RAIL](https://web.dev/articles/rail) frames the response and animation budgets. | Rebuild affected world and navigation tiles, cache route fields by destination and topology revision, update low-frequency decisions away from the 60 Hz movement pass, and retain instancing/LOD. Measure frame and edit latency at the same quality before and after each change. |

## Implementation order

The building editor portion below is historical; [building-editor-ux.md](building-editor-ux.md) supersedes its four-stage panel workflow. The agent and performance targets remain active.

1. **One understandable authoring flow.** Put building stages inside one panel, disable unavailable stages, make the canvas action and next step explicit, and keep advanced controls folded. Give roads a visible three-step flow and an accurate height/structure explanation. Verify a building, a raised road, and a tunnel by mouse and keyboard before calling the UI complete.
2. **Destination-based movement.** Add persistent trip goals to agents and a global route layer. Vehicles keep the existing IDM, lane safety, and conflict reservations; pedestrians keep the crossing permission gate and replace road-only navigation with connected walkable surfaces. A route revision invalidates cached paths, not agent identity.
3. **Bounded, reactive decisions.** Local motion runs each fixed step. Route choice, destination choice, and signal demand refresh only on topology changes or a measured delay. A nearby obstacle changes local motion immediately; a blocked route triggers a bounded replan.
4. **Performance from measurements.** A short 182-segment benchmark with 88 vehicles and 10 pedestrians measured 1.466 ms per simulation tick, including 0.581 ms for pedestrians and 0.248 ms for signals. One road edit spent 585 ms rebuilding simulation topology; a 38-road browser fixture measured a 305 ms scene rebuild. Split and reuse topology by affected region, then rerun the same benchmark and browser scene. Keep a single low-priority test job on the development machine.

## Acceptance scenarios

- A novice creates a house in the terrain, raises it, changes one facade and the roof, saves, reloads, and edits it again without hunting for a tool; an experienced player can draw an irregular multi-tier building with exact dimensions.
- A player draws a curved street, connects a ramp, raises one span over an existing road, descends into a cut and then a tunnel, sees slope and clearance before each commit, and can revise profile handles without demolishing the road.
- People walk from a meaningful origin to a destination across sidewalks and open areas, use legal crossings, avoid crowds and obstacles, and recover after a live edit. Vehicles drive toward chosen destinations, change lanes for their route or traffic, obey junction ownership, and reroute when blocked.
- Browser checks cover real gestures, desktop/mobile layout, no page errors, save/load and undo/redo. Simulation tests cover deterministic routes, collision clearance, crossing reservations and deadlock recovery. Fuzz and visual verification remain gates. Performance comparisons use the same map, population, viewport, and quality settings.
